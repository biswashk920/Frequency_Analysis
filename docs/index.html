Flood frequency analysis

Estimate how big a flood or heavy rainstorm could be, and how often it is likely to happen. Paste your yearly highest values (one per year), and the tool shows the expected size of the 2 to 500-year events using four standard methods (Gumbel, GEV, Log Normal, Log Pearson III), tells you which fits best, and lets you download everything as an Excel workbook. It all runs in your browser: your data is never uploaded.

Use one value per year: the highest flow, or the highest rainfall for one fixed duration such as 24 hours. A higher confidence level gives a wider uncertainty range. Simulations are repeated test runs used to estimate that range; 1000 is fine.

Results

Which model fits best?

    AIC rewards fit and penalises extra parameters; lower is better. A ΔAIC below 2 means two models are effectively tied. KS and RMSE are shown for reference.

    Return period estimates

    The large number is the best estimate; the smaller line beneath it is the lower and upper limit of the uncertainty range. The 100-year row is highlighted. The range reflects sampling noise within each model, not the choice between models.

    What a flood of this size looks like

    Illustration of magnitude only, not a hydraulic model. The hydrograph shape is generic, the 2-year flow is drawn at bank-full (a common rule of thumb), and water depth is scaled as flow^0.6 relative to that. Your real channel, stage and flood extent will differ.

    Frequency curves

    Gumbel and GEV use L-moments (Hosking 1990); Log Normal uses the moments of ln x; Log Pearson III uses the moments of log10 x with the bias-corrected sample skew and the exact Pearson III distribution (no regional skew). Bands are percentile intervals from a parametric bootstrap; in tests they cover the true value somewhat less often than the nominal level, and much less often if the model is wrong. KS p-values are not shown because they are optimistic when parameters are fitted to the same data. Long return periods from short records are extrapolation.
